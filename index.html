03 / 05 Must-have
UMI Sushi Air Residences: lunch (my treat!!!)
Japanese Restaurant · 4.6 ★ (74 reviews) · Units 102–103, Air Residences, Yakal St, Makati
UMI Sushi is a compact, no-frills Japanese restaurant on the ground floor of Air Residences in San Antonio, Makati. It has quietly built a loyal following for the quality of its fish, the freshness of its rolls, and the generosity of its portions. The salmon here is consistently described as buttery and melt-in-your-mouth, served in rolls or as a set with complimentary miso soup for dine-in guests. The space is small and intimate, ideal for a two-person lunch where the food does all the talking. Watching the staff prepare sushi from the front seats is half the experience. This is the heart of the itinerary and the reason the day exists.

04 / 05 Should-have
Sulok Coffee: coffee & dessert
Specialty Café · 4.8 ★ (211 reviews) · 3/F Salcedo One Center, 170 Salcedo St, Makati
Sulok Coffee is one of Makati's best-kept secrets, tucked away on the third floor of Salcedo One Center. You often smell it before you see it stepping off the elevator. The space is intimate and thoughtfully designed, with a warm, lived-in atmosphere that makes it easy to settle in and lose track of time. The coffee roster is exceptional, with signature drinks like the Tisoy (silky and balanced), Manila Sky, and Ulap worth trying. Their crème brûlée cake and pastries are a reliable pairing. It earns an impressive 4.8 stars from over 200 reviews, making it one of the highest-rated coffee spots in the Salcedo area. The walk from UMI takes roughly 10 to 12 minutes through the quiet streets of San Antonio.

4B / optional Could-have
Atrium of Makati: window shopping & walk
Commercial Building · 3.6 ★ · Makati Ave, Makati City
The Atrium of Makati is a throwback to the city's 1990s commercial golden age. The building's interior architecture retains a distinct old-world charm, with layered floors, natural light, and boutique shops that have survived the rise of newer malls nearby. It houses government agencies, a small café, and eclectic retail units that reward a slow walk-through. It is best experienced as a short detour rather than a destination. Be mindful that on-site security has received critical reviews for being unwelcoming to casual visitors. Entering with a clear intent, such as heading to the café, generally makes the experience smoother.
📍 Makati Ave, Makati City ⚠️ Closed Sat & Sun 🕗 Mon to Fri 8 AM to 7 PM 💸 Free entry (window shopping)

05 / 05 Should-have
One Ayala Terminal: P2P bus home
Transport Hub · 4.8 ★ (1,678 reviews) · Ayala Tower One, Ayala Ave, Makati · Open 24h
One Ayala Terminal is one of the most well-organised transport hubs in Metro Manila, situated at the base of Ayala Tower One along Ayala Avenue. It serves multiple P2P bus routes heading north and south, making it one of the most efficient ways to travel home from the Makati CBD without the unpredictability of flagging down a jeepney or waiting for an MRT connection. The terminal is well-reviewed for its clear signage, comfortable buses, and affordable fares. Food kiosks and a restroom are nearby. From Sulok Coffee, the terminal is about a 10 to 15-minute walk or a 5-minute Grab ride.
📍 Ayala Tower One, Ayala Ave 🚌 P2P bus routes available 💸 Est. ₱60 to ₱150 per person 🕐 Open 24 hours
